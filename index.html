Australian Agriculture

From the Paddock to the Economy

Agriculture plays an important role in Australia's economy. It provides food and commodities for domestic and international markets. From wheat farming to livestock production, agriculture varies across Australia. Different regions specialise in different agricultural industries. Production patterns continue to evolve as economic conditions change. International markets also influence Australia's agricultural landscape.

Australia's Agricultural Landscape

Where does Australia's agricultural value come from?

Australian agriculture generated almost $71 billion in gross value in 2020-21, but this economic output was not distributed evenly across the country.

New South Wales and Victoria were the two largest contributors, each generating more than $17 billion in agricultural value.

Australia's Major Crops

Which crops does Australia produce most?

Wheat dominates Australian crop production, reaching almost 32 million tonnes in 2020-21, more than double the production of barley. However, Australia's crop profile varies considerably between states.

How Australian Agriculture Has Changed

How has Australian crop production changed over time?

Australian crop production has changed considerably since the 1980s, with wheat remaining the country's dominant crop by production volume.

Canola production has expanded substantially over this period, while crops such as oats and sorghum have experienced fluctuations.

Use the interactive timeline beneath the chart to select a period and explore changes in production over time.

How has Australia's crop production mix changed?

While wheat has remained Australia's dominant crop by production volume, the relative contribution of other crops has changed considerably over time.

Canola has become an increasingly important part of Australia's crop production, while the shares of barley, oats and sorghum have fluctuated.
